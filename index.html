I build backend systems and put AI models into production. That's the work, and it's only one part of me.

I studied computer science, and somewhere in the middle I got curious about finance. I taught myself valuation and DCF, built small tools to screen stocks, and traded. I've lost more than I've won.

People told me not to, more than once. I did it anyway, because I wanted to know the thing from the inside, and now I do. A loss teaches me what reading never did.

I build systems the same way. Nobody designs the final architecture first and then watches it scale. You find it while you build, and the first version is usually wrong. My life has gone like that too. I picked several paths, none went where I planned, and what I found along the way was better than what I was aiming for.

I take things too seriously. When something I want doesn't happen, it hurts more than it should, and I'm learning to hold it lighter.

I've got a BTech degree. The paper says I learned computer science. Really? The stock market says I learned a lot more, and charged me for it.
